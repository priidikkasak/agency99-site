## READ THIS FIRST — the market is broken right now

**Nigerian ginger exports hit literally zero in 2025.** This is not a supply hiccup, it is a three-year structural collapse. Do not run outreach on this list before you have priced in the following:

| Metric | Before | Now |
|---|---|---|
| Production | 751,473 MT (2022, FAO) | 67,600 MT (2023) → ~160,000 MT (2024) → <200,000 MT est. (2025, AFEX) |
| Export revenue | ₦26.2bn (2023) | ₦6.28bn (2024) → **₦0 (2025, NBS)** |
| Export value USD | $41m (2022, Trade Map) | $11.5m (2025) |
| Domestic price, dry split | ₦1,000/kg (2022) | **₦13,000/kg (Feb 2026)** — +1,200% |
| Price per tonne | — | **₦13m–17m/MT ≈ $8,500–11,000/MT** |
| International benchmark | — | ~$2,000/MT |

### What caused it
Fungal **ginger blight** (tuber rot), present since the 2023 wet season. Wiped out >90% of harvests across southern Kaduna's producing LGAs; >2,500 ha destroyed across seven LGAs; ₦12bn in direct farmer losses. Kaduna alone was >60–70% of national supply. Seeds have been recycled for 20+ years, which is why it recurs.

### Why it hasn't recovered
NGAN's three-year **seedbank project** requires farmers to sell only 30% of harvest and replant 70%. That deliberately keeps market supply tight. Meanwhile a 100kg bag of seed ginger runs ₦560,000–950,000, so farmers are scaling down or switching crops.

### The commercial consequence
**Nigerian ginger is currently priced out of the export market.** Domestic buyers pay 4–5× the world price, so any Nigerian holder sells locally rather than exporting. This means:

1. Anyone quoting you **$1,200–2,000/MT FOB Lagos** is either quoting stale 2022 numbers, is a broker with no goods, or is running an advance-fee scam. Those figures circulate widely on SEO blogs (Kuraway, Globexia, Gavi Global etc.) and are **not the current market**.
2. Real current cost basis is ~$8,500–11,000/MT ex-Nigeria. A legitimate exporter will tell you that, and will explain why they still can't be competitive.
3. If someone offers a container at old prices, walk. That is the single clearest fraud filter available.

### What is actually worth doing
- **Value-add, not raw split.** Oleoresin and essential oil carry the price increase far better than raw rhizome. → Illaj Ginger is the only listed player doing this at scale.
- **Non-Kaduna origin.** Kano, Gombe, Bauchi, Nasarawa, Plateau, Taraba took less blight damage. → Abans (Kano FTZ), Icheel (Dawanu Market Kano).
- **Wait or forward-contract.** Federal recovery programmes (NADF's ₦1.6bn GRATE for 15,000 farmers; GVCRSP with NRCRI Umudike, tissue-culture disease-free seed) launched June 2026. ITC projects Nigeria could reach $86m/yr exports by 2030 *if* they land. Forward positions taken now are cheap relative to that.
- **Compare origins.** India, China, Peru, Thailand are unaffected. If the buyer just needs ginger, Nigeria is currently the wrong origin — the pitch only works if they specifically need high oleoresin/high pungency Nigerian material.
- **Season:** peak harvest Oct–March (NGPPMAN). Dry split availability window Nov–April. August is the wrong month to be closing volume.

## Due diligence checklist per counterparty

1. **CAC registration** — verify the RC number at Corporate Affairs Commission. No RC = no deal.
2. **NEPC registration** — mandatory to export legally. Check `nepc.gov.ng` exporter directory.
3. **NAQS certificate + phytosanitary + fumigation cert** — no EU/US customs clearance without these.
4. **Batch lab results** from SGS / Intertek / Bureau Veritas: aflatoxin B1 (EU limit 5 μg/kg), pesticide MRLs, heavy metals, moisture.
5. **Quality spec in the contract**, per NGPPMAN: moisture ≤12%, oleoresin 1.5–3.0%, impurities ≤2%, no mould/insect.
6. **Sample first.** 100–500 kg, tested independently, before any container.
7. **Payment:** LC from a top-tier bank, or 30/70 against documents. Never full advance to a first-time counterparty.
8. **Pre-shipment inspection** by SGS/Intertek. A few hundred dollars against a $200k+ container at current prices.
